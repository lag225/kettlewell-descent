The Audio

Three magnetic tapes were recovered. Two are field recordings made in Chambers One and Two before the small tape recorder Elena Kade had brought stopped working. The third is a private recording made in the Harrogate asylum in November 1962 during the short period when Dr. Vance spoke. I have transferred all three to digital in 2014 and they are provided below alongside my transcriptions. Please listen with a small volume, particularly for the asylum tape.

Tape One. Chamber One. 12 August 1962.

[recording begins mid-conversation, low room tone] VANCE. ...second reading. Compass is settling. Slightly off from magnetic north. Small variation, perhaps two degrees. WHITFIELD. Alistair. VANCE. Yes. WHITFIELD. There is a sign above the north passage. The one that goes down to Two. Did you see it. VANCE. I saw markings. WHITFIELD. E. says it is writing. VANCE. E. says a great many things. Where is she. WHITFIELD. She is at the south wall. She is sitting in the water. [pause of about six seconds] VANCE. Elena. Come away from there. KADE. [distant] I am reading. VANCE. Elena. WHITFIELD. She has been reading for twenty minutes. VANCE. What does it say. KADE. [closer] It is a welcome. WHITFIELD. A welcome. KADE. It says WELCOME STRANGER above the entrance and DEEP above the passage down. It is polite. VANCE. [pause] Are we going down. KADE. Yes. [recording ends]

Tape Two. Chamber Two. 12 August 1962.

[recording begins with the sound of scraping. possibly a boot on stone.] HARROW. [close to microphone] I am starting the camera. This is Chamber Two. The ceiling is low here. Alistair, do you want to say anything for the record. VANCE. Not particularly. HARROW. Elena. KADE. The writing continues. HARROW. In what sense. KADE. There are more symbols on the walls here. It is the same hand. The same language. The room has a name. The name is FIRST INSIDE. WHITFIELD. Inside what. KADE. Whatever the ridge is protecting. [long pause. faint dripping.] VANCE. Peter, are you all right. HARROW. I have a small headache. I would like to go up. VANCE. It is nearly evening. We will sleep here. HARROW. I would rather go up. VANCE. In the morning. We will assess in the morning. [recording ends]

Tape Three. Harrogate Asylum. 4 November 1962.

[recording begins with a nurse whispering to someone. The recorder is placed on a table.] NURSE. [quiet] He has been sitting like this for three months. Doctor Whitby thought you should hear it. He started speaking this morning at about six. [pause of about forty seconds. faint breathing.] VANCE. [barely audible] The 6th day of november. The 6th day of november. The 6th day of november. [long pause. more breathing.] VANCE. She wears our face. [the recorder is moved. sound of a chair scraping.] VANCE. [louder] She wears our face. Go down. Find her. NURSE. Alistair. VANCE. Grayish light. I do not know what to make of this. [the recorder is switched off.] [end of tape]

Ashcroft's note. The single sentence Dr. Vance is said to have spoken to the asylum staff, according to the coroner's inquest, was in a language nobody present recognised. This is not that sentence. That sentence was recorded in shorthand by Nurse Aldis on the morning of the 6th of November and preserved in the coroner's file. I have not been permitted to view the file. I have been given to understand that it consists of nine words.

The Journal

Alistair Vance kept a small notebook throughout the descent. It was recovered from the surface camp on the 16th of August 1962. Six pages had been torn from the front, apparently before the descent began; the surviving pages are transcribed below in the order they were found. Dr. Vance wrote in ink and did not correct his mistakes.

I have transcribed the entries exactly as they were written, including his date format (which he kept from an older style of English writing), his spelling (which is generally grayish and not grey), and his abbreviations. Where a passage is illegible I have written [ILLEGIBLE].

Surface Camp. The 11th day of August 1962.

Arrived at the ridge before noon. The site is exactly as M. described it in her survey. The mouth of the shaft is small, roughly a metre and a half across, opening on the west face of the outcrop. We have pitched three tents. Peter is unhappy about the wind. M. is unhappy about the light. Tonight we shared a small meal from the tins. Peter passed me the flask, which surprised me, but he said he did not want to seem unfriendly. E. was quiet. She has been quiet since Nottingham and I do not know what to make of this. We descend in the morning.

Surface Camp. The 12th day of August 1962.

Weather clear. Grayish sky at first light but the wind has dropped. We began preparations at 05:30. All equipment checked twice. Rope in good condition. The four of us descended at 09:14. Peter went first with the small camera, then M., then E., then myself. The mouth of the shaft is narrower than the survey suggested and my pack caught briefly. I took a small compass reading at the base of the first drop. The air was cool and dry. We reached what M. named Chamber One at 09:47. It is roughly circular and about thirty paces across. Water pools against the south wall. E. said immediately that she felt the room was very old. I do not know what to make of this.

Chamber Two. The 12th day of August 1962.

Descended from Chamber One through a shaft in the floor. A rope drop of about fifteen feet, straight down. Emerged into a long narrow chamber that runs almost exactly north-south, points toward magnetic north at its far end. About twenty-five paces long. Very low ceiling. The walls here are of a grayish limestone, softer than expected. E. said she has never seen this stone in this part of the country before. Peter's small camera is behaving. He shot most of the chamber.

Chamber Three. The 13th day of August 1962.

From the eastern end of Chamber Two, a passage opens into a T-junction. We named this Chamber Three. Two side tunnels head east and west from the junction, of approximately equal length, perhaps twelve paces each. The east tunnel ends in a small chamber that we will call Four when we have surveyed it. The west tunnel ends in a wall of unbroken rock. I do not know what to make of this. The survey suggested a continuation. E. has begun a small sketchbook of the marks she has found on the walls. There are marks. She says they are writing. I have my small doubts but I trust her eye.

Chamber Four. The 13th day of August 1962.

Chamber Four is small and roughly square, perhaps eight paces to a side. It lies at the east end of the Chamber Three junction. The east wall is heavily marked. E. spent two hours in this chamber alone with her sketchbook. She copied down twenty-three symbols. She would not translate any of them yet. We shared a small ration on the floor of the chamber. Peter took no drink; he says the closed air makes him uneasy. M. slept for an hour. I sat with E. She said only that we should not go deeper. I did not press her. We will decide tomorrow whether to descend below Chamber Four.

Chamber Five. The 14th day of August 1962.

E. was overruled. We descended. From the floor of Chamber Four a shaft opens south-east. It is not on M.'s survey. We roped down about twenty-five feet into a curving passage that turns south, then south-west, and opens into an oval chamber that lies almost directly beneath Chamber One at the surface. We named this Five. It is quiet and the air is different here. M. drew her map by lamplight. She was pleased with the geometry of it. She said the whole system is curving in on itself. I took a small compass reading at each turn. E. has translated a fragment. She would not tell us what.

The 15th day of August 1962.

[ILLEGIBLE - the top of this page is water-damaged] ...from the floor of Five. Peter went first this time. He would not tell us why he wanted to lead. The descent is a narrow spiral shaft, about thirty feet, that seemed to turn about itself as we went. Chamber Six is small and dark. E. read from the wall aloud in the language she has been learning. I do not know what she said. M. cried, quietly, and would not tell me why. There is a shaft in the floor of Six. I do not know what to make of this. The survey ends at Four. Peter has begun to ask questions I cannot answer. He wants to know how many of us came down. I said four. He said four including who.

[UNDATED. LAST ENTRY.]

Chamber Seven. Circular. The sign on the wall is one E. has seen before. She said we are already inside. I do not know what to make of this.

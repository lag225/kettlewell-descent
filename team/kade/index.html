Elena Kade

linguist. informal. not on the grant roster.

This page exists for the sake of completeness. Elena Kade was not a listed member of the Kettlewell expedition. She was not funded by the Sheffield society and she is not named in any official document from the time. I have included her here because I do not believe the record is complete without her, and because the materials she left behind are difficult to explain if one insists she was not present.

She was twenty-six in the summer of 1962. She was reading for a doctorate in linguistic anthropology at Manchester. Her thesis concerned the survival of pre-Norse writing systems in the north of England, an area of study that was not popular then and is not popular now. She had known M. Whitfield since their overlapping year at Nottingham, where the two had been very close.

She travelled with M. to Kettlewell in the first week of August 1962. Villagers at the pub in Kettlewell village remembered a young woman with M. in the days before the descent, though the name they remembered was garbled. She is present in one photograph in Peter Harrow's possession, standing behind the three named team members at the mouth of the shaft. I have chosen not to publish that photograph out of respect for her memory.

She went down with the others. This is my belief, based on the journal entries preserved on this site and on the notebook of translation sketches she left in the surface camp, which is now the Kade Notebook.

She was never mentioned in the inquiry. She had no family that I have been able to find in more than fifty years of searching. If you knew her, please sign the guestbook.

Margaret Whitfield

cartographer. surface mapping. Vance's fiancée. lost.

M. was twenty-eight in August 1962. She had trained as a cartographer at Nottingham. In the year before the descent she was employed part-time producing survey maps for the National Coal Board. Her father had been a surveyor before her. She was, by all accounts, a small, quick woman with an easy laugh.

She had descended into caves twice before, both times with Vance, and had shown a talent for below-ground mapping that Vance mentioned in his correspondence with the Sheffield society. She was primarily responsible for the surface survey of the Kettlewell shaft in the spring of 1962. The map she produced is not available; the National Coal Board withdrew it from public view after her death was recorded.

She was engaged to Vance and, according to her mother, spent a great deal of the summer of 1962 arranging their October wedding. Her closest friend from Nottingham, a young woman she had roomed with at university, is said to have been staying with her for the two weeks before the descent, though this is not confirmed by any surviving letter.

She was never recovered. She was declared dead in absentia in 1970.

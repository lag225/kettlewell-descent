Peter Harrow

cameraman. documentary filmmaker. lost.

Peter Harrow was thirty-one in August 1962. He worked in London as a freelance documentary cameraman, with credits on several small films for the BBC and one for Granada. He had been friends with Vance since their overlapping years at Sheffield, where Harrow had briefly read English before switching to a photography apprenticeship.

He was, by his father's account, a small quiet man who cared a great deal about his equipment and very little about being seen. He had never previously descended into a cave. Vance recruited him to Kettlewell specifically to film the descent, and Harrow bought a new sixteen-millimetre camera for the purpose, along with three film cans he estimated would last four days of intermittent shooting.

Peter did not drink. He had, as his father put it, a small aversion to the smell of spirits from an incident in his childhood that he never discussed. This is worth mentioning only because it comes up later.

The film he shot was recovered from the surface camp. Two of the three cans were exposed. The third was empty. The recovered footage is preserved in the film section.

He was never recovered. He was declared dead in absentia in 1970.

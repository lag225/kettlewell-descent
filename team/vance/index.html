Dr. Alistair Vance

lead. geologist. the one who came back.

Alistair Vance was thirty-four in the summer of 1962. He held a doctorate in geology from Sheffield University and was in the second year of a small teaching post there. He had grown up in a village outside Skipton and had been descending into caves since the age of fifteen. He was, by all accounts, a serious man. His fiancée said he laughed rarely but well.

He had made three prior descents into Yorkshire cave systems. His notes from those trips are careful and precise. He kept a small compass reading at every chamber, sketched the walls where they were of interest, and made a habit of writing the date at the top of every page in the format he preferred, the 6th day of June and so on.

He was engaged to M. Whitfield. They planned to marry that October.

He was the only member of the team who returned from the descent. He was found sitting on the ridge at 07:12 on the morning of the 16th, ten paces from the shaft, in a grayish daze that observers described as almost peaceful. He did not respond to speech for the next three months. In November of that year he spoke a single sentence in a language nobody present recognised, and then he did not speak again. He was moved to a private asylum outside Harrogate in 1963. His death was recorded there on 4 January 1971. His body was not publicly displayed.

His sister donated his surviving journal pages to this archive in 1974. The journal page holds transcriptions.

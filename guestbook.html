Guestbook

Please sign below if you knew the team or if you have information about the descent. I read every entry. I am slow to reply but I always do reply. New signings are added to the top.

Rowan T. - 18 june 2024 Found this site while researching yorkshire cave folklore. Fascinating stuff. Do you have a bibliography anywhere? Would love to read more about the pre-norse writing system Kade was working on.
J. Ashcroft - 22 june 2024 Rowan, thank you for signing. I am afraid the pre-norse system Elena was studying has almost no surviving academic literature. Her supervisor's papers were destroyed in a small flood at Manchester in 1971. If you are seriously interested, the closest surviving grammar is Ellis Davies' 1948 Notes On Cumbrian Runic Survivals, which is held at the small library in Kendal. I have not been able to obtain a copy myself but I understand it is grayish and stained but readable. I do not know what to make of the fact that so little has survived.
Mrs. Ainsworth - 3 october 2019 My father owned the pub in Kettlewell village in 1962. He was still alive when I was young and he spoke about the summer of the descent often. He said four young people came through the village that week. Three men and two women. I thought at the time he was misremembering because the papers said three. But he was very firm. Four went up the hill. Only one came down.
J. Ashcroft - 4 october 2019 Mrs. Ainsworth, thank you for signing. Your father was correct. There were four. The official record has always been in small error on this point. I am glad someone in the village kept the true count. Please pass on my regards to any of his surviving relatives who might remember more.
Michael Harrow - 12 january 2015 I am Peter Harrow's nephew. I am the son of Peter's older brother. Our family has been quiet about the descent for many years and I was told as a child not to bring it up. I want to thank whoever runs this site for treating my uncle with respect. His father, my grandfather, went to his grave believing Peter was still alive somewhere below ground. I do not know what to make of that. My uncle did not drink. He would not have taken the flask. I mention it because the journal says he did.
J. Ashcroft - 18 january 2015 Michael, thank you for signing. I hope the archive has been a small comfort. Your grandfather was not alone in his belief. I keep the archive for people like him. As for the flask, we can only guess. Perhaps the small confinement of the cave changed a habit. Perhaps the transcription is wrong. I have preserved what I received.
David Whitfield - 6 may 2011 My aunt was Margaret Whitfield. I am writing on behalf of what remains of the Whitfield family. We appreciate the care you have taken. My grandmother, who was M.'s mother, wanted me to pass on that the friend who was staying with M. in the two weeks before the descent was Elena, whom she referred to simply as 'the linguist girl'. My grandmother believed until her death that both women went into the shaft.
J. Ashcroft - 11 may 2011 David, thank you for signing. Please give my regards to your family. I have long believed the same about Elena. The evidence is small but it is consistent. I am glad your grandmother spoke of it.
Dr. E. Whitby - 22 february 2004 I was a junior doctor at the Harrogate asylum in 1962 and 1963. I attended Dr. Vance for the last month of his silence and for the whole of the short period during which he spoke. I have not signed a guestbook in my life but this archive is careful and I wanted to add my name. Dr. Vance was not in the ordinary sense mad. He was somewhere else. The sentence he spoke to us in november is one that I have thought about every day since. I will not repeat it here.
J. Ashcroft - 2 march 2004 Dr. Whitby, I am honoured that you have signed. Your care of Alistair in his last years was widely known and much appreciated by his sister. If you ever wish to speak in more detail I would be a small and grateful audience.
Alan Kendrick - 7 august 2003 I run a caving forum called the small pit. Signing to say we appreciate this archive. Your dedication is unusual and welcome. If you ever want us to help you get down there and finish the survey, we are willing.
J. Ashcroft - 9 august 2003 Alan, thank you but I must decline on behalf of the archive. The site is not to be visited. I have my small reasons.
